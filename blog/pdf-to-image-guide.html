Why turn a PDF into images?

A few common reasons: posting slides to social media, embedding pages in a blog post, sharing a page with someone who doesn't have a PDF reader, using a page as a thumbnail or avatar, sending a diagram into a tool that only accepts PNG/JPG. Once you've got the image, you can crop, annotate, or upload it anywhere.

Quality matters more than people think — a low-resolution export makes text look fuzzy, and a high-resolution one wastes bandwidth. The sweet spot for screenshots and social posts is 144–200 DPI; for print, go to 300.

Method 1: Browser-based converter (free, nothing uploaded)

Open the PDF to PNG / JPG converter, drop your PDF in, pick the format and resolution, and click Convert. Each page becomes an image, and you download the whole batch as a single ZIP.

Best for: sensitive documents (contracts, internal slides), batch jobs of a few dozen pages, situations where you don't want the file going anywhere.

Everything is rendered locally using pdf.js — the file is parsed in your browser and the images are generated on the fly. Nothing is uploaded.

Method 2: macOS Preview (free, already on your Mac)

Open the PDF in Preview → File → Export → select PNG or JPEG → choose resolution → Save. It's built in and handles most files well.

Downside: one page at a time. For a 30-page deck you'll be clicking through all of them.

Method 3: Adobe Acrobat Pro (paid)

File → Export To → Image. Lets you batch-export every page at once with consistent resolution, and you can apply compression to keep file sizes down. Costs $22.99/month, cloud by default.

Method 4: Take a screenshot (free, lossy)

Open the PDF, press Cmd-Shift-4 (Mac) or Win-Shift-S (Windows), drag a rectangle around the page. Done.

Best for one or two pages; not practical for a 50-page deck. Resolution is fixed to whatever your screen is rendering at, so it's lower than you'd get from a real export.

Picking a format

  • PNG — lossless, larger file. Best for diagrams, screenshots with text, anything where you can't tolerate blur.
  • JPEG — lossy but much smaller. Fine for photos and screenshots of prose. Avoid for crisp diagrams (edges get halos).
  • WebP — smaller than JPEG at similar quality, supported everywhere modern. Best when you care about file size.

Common gotchas

"The text is pixelated"

Increase the export DPI. Default 72 DPI is screen-only — anything you'd publish or print needs at least 144 DPI. Our browser-based tool defaults to 200 DPI which is a good middle ground.

"It's a huge ZIP"

Two options: lower the DPI, or switch from PNG to JPEG. A 20-page deck exported at 200 DPI is roughly 4 MB as PNG and roughly 800 KB as JPEG. Visual loss is minimal for screenshots.

"Transparent backgrounds came out white"

PDFs sometimes have transparent backgrounds that disappear on export. That's usually fine for screenshots, but if you need a transparent PNG, use a tool that specifically supports it (most browser converters don't).

"I only need one page"

Use our tool and just delete the others from the ZIP, or take a screenshot of the single page.

Privacy

Our converter renders every page locally with pdf.js and bundles them into a ZIP in your browser's memory. Nothing leaves your device. If the PDF contains a draft deck or an unreleased financial report, this is the only way to do it safely.
