How to OCR a scanned PDF
— make it searchable in seconds

A scanned PDF is just a pile of pictures. You can't Ctrl+F, you can't copy, you can't select. OCR fixes that by reading the text out of the images. Here's the fastest way to do it — without uploading your file anywhere.

What OCR actually does

OCR (Optical Character Recognition) looks at each page image and figures out which pixels form letters, then maps those letters to real text characters. The output is a PDF where the text is selectable — you can search it, copy it, and highlight it. The original layout stays put; only an invisible text layer is added underneath.

The fast way (runs on your device)

Open our PDF OCR tool, drop your scanned file, pick a language, and click Run. It uses an on-device engine (Tesseract.js) that processes everything inside your browser tab. Nothing is uploaded to a server.

Best for: Contracts, receipts, invoices, book scans, lecture notes — anything where you later need to find a phrase or pull a quote.

Supported languages include English, Chinese (simplified & traditional), Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Portuguese, Russian, and 20+ more.

Method 2: Adobe Acrobat (paid, cloud-based)

Acrobat Pro → Tools → Scan & OCR → Recognize Text. It's accurate, but it costs $22.99/month and sends your document to Adobe's cloud. For a confidential scan, that's a privacy trade-off.

Method 3: Google Drive (free, but uploads)

Upload the PDF to Drive, right-click → Open with → Google Docs. Drive OCRs it for free. The catch: the file leaves your account and the formatting often shifts (tables break, columns collapse). Fine for a quick quote, not for a polished doc.

Common gotchas

"It misses half the words"

OCR quality follows image quality. Scan at 300 DPI or higher, and make sure pages are straight. Our tool auto-skips blurry input, but a clean source beats any setting.

"It mixes up 0 and O, 1 and l"

Typical for low-res or unusual fonts. Re-scan at higher DPI, or pick the exact language (don't leave it on "auto" for a single-language doc).

"Handwriting doesn't work"

Printed text OCRs well; handwriting usually doesn't. If the source is handwritten, expect spotty results regardless of tool.

Privacy

Our OCR runs locally with Tesseract.js. Your PDF never leaves your browser. For sensitive scans — client decks, signed contracts, medical paperwork — that's the safe choice.
