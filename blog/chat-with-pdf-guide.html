What "chat with a PDF" actually means

An AI reads your PDF, builds a temporary index of every page, and answers your questions by pointing back to the exact passages it used. You ask plain-language questions ("what's the termination clause?" or "summarise section 3"), and it returns answers with citations like "page 12, paragraph 2" so you can verify.

Useful for contracts you don't want to read cover-to-cover, research papers, multi-chapter reports, and long manuals. Less useful for short documents — reading them is faster than asking.

Method 1: Browser-based, runs on-device (free, nothing uploaded)

Open the Chat with PDF tool, drop your file in, and start typing. It runs the model locally using WebAssembly and your browser's storage. The PDF is parsed, indexed, and answered against entirely inside your tab — no server round-trip.

Best for: legal contracts, medical records, internal reports, anything where "this file left my computer" is not an option you can afford.

Because the file never leaves your device, you can paste a 200-page contract and ask "list every liability clause" without worrying about it training someone else's model or showing up in a future data breach.

Method 2: ChatGPT Plus (paid, cloud)

Open ChatGPT Plus → "Work with Apps" or the file upload button → upload your PDF → ask. It uses GPT-4 with retrieval over your file. Excellent at synthesis, decent at citing the right page.

Costs $20/month and uploads the document to OpenAI's servers. Files are stored for a retention period; you can delete them in settings, but they may be processed for abuse review.

Method 3: NotebookLM (free, Google account required)

Google's NotebookLM accepts PDFs, Google Docs, and pasted text. It answers with citations pulled from your sources — and crucially, it answers only from the sources you give it, so it won't hallucinate from the open web.

Free with a Google account. Files are stored in Google's cloud.

Method 4: Claude.ai (paid, cloud)

Upload a PDF to claude.ai (free tier accepts small files; Pro is $20/month for large ones). Claude is especially good at long documents — its 200K context window handles 500-page reports without chunking.

Common gotchas

"The answer contradicts itself"

Long PDFs confuse even the best models. If the file is over ~100 pages, prefer a tool that indexes the document (browser-based or NotebookLM) over a model that just dumps the whole thing into context. Indexing means it can re-read a specific section when answering, rather than guessing from a blurry memory of the whole thing.

"It cites page 7 but page 7 says something different"

Page numbers in PDFs are physical — "page 7" is the 7th sheet of paper, not always the 7th numbered page in the document. Tools sometimes report the logical page number and sometimes the physical one. Always open the cited page and read for yourself before relying on the answer.

"It won't read scanned PDFs"

A scan is an image — there's no text to index. Run it through PDF OCR first to add a text layer, then chat with it.

"It refuses to answer my question"

Most cloud tools have safety filters that flag legal, medical, and certain business documents. Browser-based tools don't, because there's nothing to enforce against — the file and the model are both on your machine.

Privacy in plain English

If you wouldn't paste the document into a public Slack channel, don't upload it to a cloud AI tool. Browser-based tools — including ours — run the whole pipeline locally, so the file stays yours. That's the only way to be sure.
