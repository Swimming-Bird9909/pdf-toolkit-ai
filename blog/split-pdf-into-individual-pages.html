What "split into individual pages" means

Splitting a PDF into individual pages means taking one multi-page file and turning it into separate one-page PDFs — page 1 becomes file-1.pdf, page 2 becomes file-2.pdf, and so on. It is the opposite of merge. People do it to email just one page, print a single sheet, or feed pages one at a time into another workflow.

The fast way (runs in your browser)

Open our Split PDF tool, drop your file, choose "extract single pages" and type the page numbers you want (e.g. 3, 7, 15) — or pick "every 1 page" to break the whole document into individual pages. Click Process. It uses pdf-lib and runs entirely on your device. Nothing uploads.

Best for: Contracts, invoices, scanned receipts, report chapters — anything where you only need a slice of the original.

Method 2: Adobe Acrobat (paid)

Acrobat → Organize Pages → Split. Accurate, but $22.99/month and your file goes to Adobe's cloud. For confidential docs that is a privacy trade-off.

Method 3: Preview (Mac, free, local)

Open the PDF in Preview, select thumbnails, drag them out to the desktop. Free and local, but fiddly for more than a few pages and no batch mode.

Common gotchas

"I only want pages 5-10"

Type 5-10 in the range box — you do not have to extract them one by one. Mix ranges and singles: 1-3, 5-12, 20.

"The output is huge"

Splitting is lossless, so each page keeps its original quality. If the source was a 50MB scan, 50 one-page files will total ~50MB. That is expected.

"I need to reorder too"

Split first, then use Merge PDF to recombine the extracted pages in any order.

Privacy

Our splitter runs locally with pdf-lib. Your PDF never leaves your browser. For signed contracts, medical records, or financial statements, that is the safe choice.
