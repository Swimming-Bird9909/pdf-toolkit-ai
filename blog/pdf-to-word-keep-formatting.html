Why formatting breaks (and how to keep it)

A PDF is a "fixed" format — it describes where every character sits on the page. Word is a "flowing" format — it reflows text as you edit. Converting means teaching Word where paragraphs, headings, and tables begin and end. Good converters reconstruct that structure; bad ones dump everything as one text blob.

The 30-second version

  1. Open our free PDF to Word converter.
  2. Drop your PDF. Extraction runs in your browser — no upload.
  3. Download the .docx. Open in Word, Google Docs, or LibreOffice.
  4. Tidy any complex table in 2 minutes.

What converts cleanly

  • Headings & paragraphs — map to Word styles so you get a real outline.
  • Bullet & numbered lists — preserved as native lists.
  • Simple tables — become editable Word tables you can sort and resize.
  • Fonts — standard fonts embed; custom fonts fall back gracefully.

What needs a quick cleanup

  • Multi-column layouts (newsletters, academic papers) may arrive as single-column.
  • Complex bordered tables sometimes convert as text — re-table in 1 minute.
  • Scanned PDFs have no text layer; run OCR first, then convert.

Step-by-step to maximize fidelity

  1. Start from a digital PDF (one with selectable text), not a scan.
  2. If it's a scan, OCR it first with our PDF OCR tool.
  3. Convert, then open in Word and use "Automatically fix the layout" if offered.
  4. For tables, select the pasted text and use Word's "Convert Text to Table".

Privacy check

Text extraction uses pdf.js in your browser. The document never leaves your device — open DevTools → Network and you'll see zero upload traffic. Important for the NDAs, financials, and client docs people convert most.

FAQ

Will the layout survive?
Text, headings, and most formatting convert well. Very complex multi-column or custom-font layouts may need light cleanup.

Is my PDF uploaded for conversion?
No. Extraction runs locally; the file never leaves your browser.

Do tables convert correctly?
Simple tables become native Word tables. Complex bordered tables may arrive as text you re-table quickly.

Can I convert a scanned PDF?
Not directly — run OCR first to get a text layer, then convert to Word.
