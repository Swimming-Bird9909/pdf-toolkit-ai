Why "compress to 100KB" is the search everyone does

Most PDF compressors let you pick "small / medium / large" and hope for the best. But real life gives you a hard number: upload forms cap at 100KB, email providers reject over 10–25MB, and some government portals demand "under 500KB". You need a tool that hits the number, not a vibe. That's what target-size compression does.

The 30-second version

  1. Open our free PDF compressor.
  2. Drop your PDF. It stays in your browser — nothing is uploaded.
  3. Pick a target: 100KB, 200KB, 500KB, or type a custom size.
  4. Click Compress. Download the result and check the size.

Method 1: Lossless compression (best for text PDFs)

If your PDF is mostly text (reports, essays, contracts), lossless mode re-packs the file without touching a single pixel. Text stays razor-sharp, and the file often drops 30–60% just from cleaner object storage.

  • Best for: contracts, essays, ebooks, any document where text must stay crisp.
  • Trade-off: limited savings if the file is already optimized.

Method 2: Lossy compression (best for image-heavy PDFs)

Scanned documents and slide decks are giant photos in disguise. Lossy mode re-encodes those images at a lower quality to hit your target. A 5MB scan drops to 100KB easily.

  • Best for: scanned PDFs, presentations, portfolios, photo-heavy reports.
  • Trade-off: some image sharpness is sacrificed — preview before sending.

How to guarantee "under 100KB"

  1. Set the target to 100KB (not "small").
  2. If the result is still over, switch to lossy mode and lower the image quality slider.
  3. Still too big? Split the PDF and send only the pages the recipient needs.
  4. Remove embedded fonts you don't need via Remove Metadata first.

Real-world scenarios

"The job portal rejects my 2MB resume"

Set target 100KB, lossy on. Your one-page resume compresses to well under the limit while staying readable on screen.

"Gmail bounced my 15MB contract"

Compress to 500KB lossless first. If it's scanned, use lossy. Then attach — it sails through.

"The government form says under 1MB"

Target 900KB to leave headroom. Never aim exactly at the limit; aim 10% under.

Privacy check

Compression runs entirely in your browser with pdf-lib. Your file never touches a server — open DevTools → Network and you'll see zero upload traffic. That matters for the tax forms, IDs, and contracts people compress most.

FAQ

Will compressing to 100KB make text blurry?
Lossless mode keeps text perfectly sharp. Only lossy image recompression affects sharpness, and you control the quality slider.

Can I set a custom size like 250KB?
Yes — type any target. The tool iterates to get as close as possible without exceeding it.

Is there a file-size limit?
Practically ~500MB (browser memory). For bigger files, compress in batches.

Does it work on a phone?
Yes, on any modern mobile browser. Large files are faster on desktop.
