Why does the site switch back to Chinese?
(And how we fixed it)

You tap EN, the page turns English, you refresh — and it's Chinese again. If that sounds familiar, you're not imagining it. Here's exactly what was happening and what we changed.

📅 August 12, 2026 · 4 min read

The short version

Your language choice was being saved in a part of the browser called localStorage. Some browsers — mainly Safari, Firefox, and aggressive privacy/anti-tracking modes — restrict or silently drop localStorage writes. So your "English" choice never actually got saved, and the next time the page loaded it fell back to the default language.

We now save your choice in a cookie instead. Cookies survive those restrictions, so English (or Chinese) now sticks across refreshes, tabs, and most privacy settings. If you were hitting this, just refresh once more and pick your language — it'll stay.

What you were seeing

  • You click EN in the top-right switcher. The page shows English. Good.
  • You refresh the page (or pull-to-refresh on mobile). It's Chinese again.
  • Clicking 中文 always stays Chinese — only English "reverts".
  • It's worst in Safari and Firefox, and "works fine" in Chrome.

That last point is the big clue. Chrome is more permissive about localStorage, so the choice stuck there. Safari and Firefox are stricter, so the choice got lost — and only the English choice was affected because the fallback language happens to be Chinese.

Why it happened (plain English)

Every page on the site exists in two flavors with two addresses:

  • English: wezzik.com/... (no /zh/ prefix)
  • Chinese: wezzik.com/zh/... (with the /zh/ prefix)

When you switch languages, two things happen: the site takes you to the matching address and remembers your pick so future visits open in that language. The "remember" part was the problem.

We stored that memory in localStorage. It works great in Chrome. But in Safari/Firefox (and in any browser with "Block all cookies" or strong tracking protection), localStorage can be blocked or wiped — and our code quietly swallowed the error instead of failing loudly. Result: the memory was empty, so the site assumed you wanted the default language (Chinese) and redirected you there.

Because the default is Chinese, only the English choice ever looked "broken" — Chinese was both the fallback and the saved value, so it always appeared correct.

What we changed

  1. Your language choice is now saved in a cookie, not localStorage. Cookies are the web's standard, reliable memory — they aren't blocked by the same privacy settings that break localStorage. The choice persists for a year and works across every browser we tested.
  2. The address is always the source of truth. Even with no memory at all, the URL you're on decides the language. Bookmark wezzik.com for English or wezzik.com/zh/ for Chinese and it can never "flip" on you.
  3. We removed a stuck loading state on the switcher so the click always feels instant.

No accounts, no upload, no tracking — just a tiny cookie that says "this visitor prefers English".

How to make sure English sticks for you

  1. Refresh once after this update so your browser picks up the new code.
  2. Pick EN in the switcher. It now saves to a cookie and survives refreshes.
  3. Bookmark the English URL (wezzik.com) if you want to be 100% safe — the address itself is English.
  4. If you use "Block all cookies" or a very strict anti-tracking extension, allow cookies for wezzik.com. The language memory lives in a first-party cookie; without it, the site can't remember any setting (theme included).

FAQ

Is my PDF data affected by this?
No. The language setting is completely separate from file processing. Every tool still runs 100% in your browser — your files never leave your device.

Why was it fine in Chrome but not Safari/Firefox?
Chrome allows localStorage even under stricter defaults; Safari and Firefox (and most privacy modes) restrict it. We switched to cookies precisely because cookies are respected across all of them.

Does this mean you're tracking me?
No. The only thing the cookie stores is your language preference (en or zh). There's no identifier, no analytics tie-in, and nothing is sent anywhere except back to our own server to serve the right page.

What if I want Chinese back?
Click 中文 in the switcher. It saves the same way and sticks just as reliably.
