The 30-second fix

  1. Open our free PDF compressor.
  2. Drop your PDF.
  3. Pick the "Email-ready" preset (~300 KB).
  4. Click Compress. Download. Attach.

That's it. Files are processed in your browser — no upload, no signup.

Why PDFs get so big

Most of a PDF's size comes from embedded images. A 50-page report with full-resolution screenshots can hit 50 MB+ even though the actual text is tiny. When you compress, you're really recompressing those embedded images at a lower JPEG/WebP quality — which is invisible to the eye at typical screen sizes.

Three real scenarios

Scenario 1: Job application forms that demand a 1 MB PDF

Many HR systems reject uploads over 1-2 MB. Use the "Compress to 500 KB" preset. If it still rejects, try 200 KB.

Scenario 2: Sending a 40-page deck through Outlook

Outlook's default limit is 20 MB. The "Email-ready" preset targets ~300 KB which leaves plenty of room for the email body and other attachments.

Scenario 3: Sharing a scanned book chapter (image-heavy)

For pure scans, the lossless preset usually saves 30-50%. If you need more aggressive compression, use 100 KB preset — text becomes slightly fuzzy but readable.

What "no quality loss" really means

Our "Reduce size, no quality loss" option re-encodes images at 85% quality with no DPI reduction. Text stays crisp (because we never re-rasterize text layers). It's the safest option when you don't know the recipient's expectations.

Privacy check

Open DevTools → Network → upload a PDF through our tool. You'll see zero file upload traffic. That's because everything runs locally with pdf-lib and pdf.js. Your resume, your medical report, your divorce decree — all stay on your device.

FAQ

Will compression break my links/bookmarks?
No — we preserve the document outline and link annotations.

What about password-protected PDFs?
Unlock them in Acrobat first, then compress.

Will OCR'd text remain searchable?
Yes, the text layer is preserved.
